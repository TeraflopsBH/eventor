## 1. Executive objective and product rules

Deliver a unified, configurable Communication and Delivery Center for Eventor organizations. A venue provider can communicate with booking customers without an operational event; an organizer can invite participants, collect RSVP, distribute passes and send approved updates; a hybrid organization can independently do both. Authorized operators can choose electronic or physical distribution methods according to purpose, recipient eligibility, organization policy, provider capabilities and actual cost. Eventor must support persons who have no email address, telephone, messaging account, smartphone or internet connection. Sending a message is an operational action, not a mandatory dependency of every business workflow.

Separate domain intent, approved content, audience, transport, delivery evidence and recipient action. Invitation, RSVP, eligibility, admission credential, physical attendance, booking offer/acceptance, payment and event lifecycle remain independent facts. A sent invitation is not an accepted RSVP. A delivered offer is not a booking confirmation. A read notice is not acceptance of a cancellation, a task or a collaboration grant. A print job does not confirm handover, admission or payment. No delivery adapter may alter any of those business facts. Domain owners initiate and authorize their own commands; CID transports or records distribution and exposes purpose-limited evidence. Where a recipient acts, the authoritative domain endpoint verifies the exact capability and explicit action.

A shared delivery service does not imply a global recipient directory, shared credential, shared provider account or cross-tenant access. The normal organization context, explicit event/booking/task scopes, field-level restrictions, active grants, purpose and recipient authority apply. Platform-level service administration is a separate bounded context, not inherited access to guest or customer communications. A provider-only booking need not create event/guest records. Cross-party notices contain the deliberately shared projection, not an organization's underlying booking, guest, commercial or security data.

**Deployment independence:** native Linux and Docker Compose remain first-class. Local/intranet-only operation, local capture and manual distribution must work without paid services. Public guest links require reachable approved origin and TLS; external provider access, DNS, mail domains and callbacks depend on site networking. Home ISP limitations are a test environment characteristic, not product architecture. Do not require incoming SMTP, a hosted SaaS control plane, a specific port, commercial gateway or cloud dependency for core operation. No mandatory public webhook for offline/manual operation; supported polling/manual reconciliation when callbacks cannot be configured, with honest limitations.

**Out of scope:** general-purpose marketing automation/CRM, bulk contact harvesting, social network, private inbox scraping, arbitrary provider scripts, public mail relay, spam tools, unofficial personal-account browser automation, call recording/transcription, unrestricted chatbot conversations, payment processing or platform invoice issuance. Recipient contact consent is not inferred from appearance in the Registry. Do not use the system for unsolicited mass messaging. No guaranteed message delivery, read or provider-side deletion. Optional future channels need reviewed adapters, not arbitrary custom code in the UI.

## 3. CID-01 — Communication purpose catalogue and owning domains

Purpose is a stable, versioned allowlist, not free-form strings used for authorization. Each purpose specifies owner command, permitted recipient subject types, allowed content bindings, action-capability type, sensitivity, mandatory or optional policy, eligible channels, approval, expiry, retention, and whether automated scheduling/fallback is permissible. Extend catalogue via reviewed migration/contract; no user-created arbitrary SQL recipient selection or action code. One event may enable invitations without admission, or RSVP without portal; a channel can be disabled without deleting existing history or cancelling event.

| Purpose family | Owner and audience | Examples / restrictions |
|---|---|---|
| Organization enrollment, security and support | PAC/OAC identity/membership services; verified intended account or authorized admin | Enrollment, password/security alerts, account recovery and support receipts; use dedicated identity capability, not guest token; offline protected host bootstrap/recovery remains. Generic notification worker cannot reset credentials. Never send passwords or MFA seeds. |
| Booking inquiry and offer | Booking domain; requester/contracting party/contact explicitly authorized | Acknowledgement, clarification, revised offer, expiry reminder, customer decision, confirmation and instructions. Public links scoped to exact offer/action; no implicit event participation. Prices/policies only from pinned permitted snapshot; never disclose unrelated customers. |
| Cancellation, amendment and disruption | Booking provider or organizer per distinct decision/grant | Customer request receipt, authorized decision/effective cancellation, linked organizer notice, replacement date/reconfirmation. Public reason only; delivery cannot execute decision, cancel event, refund or change reservation. |
| Guest invitation / RSVP | Event participation and invitation domain | Issue, distribute, reminder, resend, explicit reissue, response confirmation, reconfirmation; guest-specific purpose/expiry. No response is not declined; delivery does not reset answer. |
| Admission / guest portal / seating | Admission, portal and allocation domain (#105–107/AGIS) | Separate pass distribution, guest-safe destination, programme and published seat change; no other guest positions or internal reason; no attendance on lookup/send. |
| Event programme, operational announcement | Event/session owner | Arrival instructions, approved public schedule/venue notices, accessibility directions, reminders, closing notice; select private vs public projection. A passive display is a publication surface, not recipient delivery evidence. |
| Staff and vendor coordination | Task owner and authorized recipient role/grant | Assignment, due date, request, revision, acknowledgement, escalation, readiness. No broad Registry disclosure or restricted external-prerequisite evidence; coordination not official inspection/certification. |
| Partner collaboration | Grantor/recipient party scoped to grant lifecycle | Proposal/review prompt, acceptance/revocation notification, explicit permitted booking/event link or disruption notice. Message cannot grant access or accept proposal. |
| Post-event correspondence | Event owner; permitted audience | Thank-you, approved feedback form, certificate/document notice; purpose and preference check; no implied marketing authorization. |
| System operation | PAC/OAC operational owner | Queue health, quota/credential expiry, backup incident and readiness; sanitize no secret/content, distinguish staff inbox from external send. |

Some security/contract notices may have a separately lawful necessity, not blanket consent bypass. Default deny unsupported purposes; organization policy cannot bypass law/provider limits. Critical business decision always remains recorded and visible even when no channel eligible; show contact/notification obligation and safe follow-up. Staff must be able to complete non-communication business work independently.

## 4. CID-02 — Channel and provider capability registry

Distinguish channel (email, SMS, WhatsApp, Viber, Telegram, web_push, in_app, manual_share, print, postal, handover, telephone) from provider (SMTP relay, email API, official messaging platform, local renderer, operator) and endpoint/account/sender identity. Multiple providers may serve same channel. A provider transport selection cannot change a recipient's contact or legal eligibility. Define typed capability metadata: supports plain text, HTML, multipart, attachments/media and size/type limits; structured template and approval, localization, sender kinds, destination kinds, opt-in prerequisites, message-window rules, batch behavior, idempotency support/window, callback signature and status types, polling, cancellation, rate limits, cost units/currency, region/registration restrictions, link length and content rules. Each capability version pinned to routing/validation evidence. Unknown is unsupported, not permissive. Official docs and actual account evidence required at scheduling; provider policy/version changes trigger readiness revalidation.

| Channel | Candidate adapter pattern | Evidence / limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Email SMTP | Existing Nodemailer factory; TLS, approved credentials, local relay | SMTP acceptance only; DSN/provider callbacks only when verified. No read/open inference. |
| Email HTTP | Versioned first-class API adapters e.g. Resend initially; other candidates Brevo, Mailgun, SendGrid, SES, Gmail API, Microsoft Graph | Per-account authentication, permissions, verified sender, provider idempotency semantics and callbacks; no blanket compatibility claim. |
| SMS | Official gateway API, e.g. Twilio or regional provider | E.164 or approved regional routing, registration, Unicode and segment cost, status receipts if supported; no read confirmation. |
| WhatsApp | Official Business Platform or approved BSP | Registered business sender, recipient opt-in, approved templates/categories/windows as required by account/provider, media/link constraints. No consumer session automation. |
| Viber | Official Business Messages provider or official subscribed-bot adapter | Business route and bot subscription IDs are different; phone contact alone not bot enrollment. Delivery/read only supported evidence. |
| Telegram (optional) | Official Bot API | Guest initiates/binds bot conversation; no unsolicited lookup by phone; use verified chat reference; no read receipt assumption. |
| Web push | Standards-based push service with VAPID, encrypted payload and subscription | Browser permission + verified subscription; no forced prompt; push-service acceptance not human read; notification text minimal. |
| In-app | Existing scoped realtime/inbox projection | Only active entitled account/session or guest portal with valid capability; no external delivery claim. |
| Manual digital share | Explicit secure copy/download/handoff | Operator evidence only; no automated WhatsApp/Viber delivery claim from opening share sheet. |
| Print / handover / postal | Existing TDS and AGIS print/document paths + manual record | Rendered/printed/dispatched/handed-over separate; carrier tracking when verified. |
| Telephone | Operator task and structured outcome | Not dialer or call recording; actor records contacted/left message/no answer and separate domain response if authorized. |

The minimum supported product includes independent manual, print, in-app and configurable SMTP even without external account. CID proposes official HTTP email, OAuth-capable email, SMS, WhatsApp and Viber adapter coverage for the complete scope, with web push supported as opt-in; Telegram remains optional extension. For each advertised supported provider, require actual provider/account sandbox evidence as applicable. An event/tenant may choose none or subset. Release-wide omission of a required channel needs explicit scope decision, not fake “supported” toggle. Provider examples are candidates, not promises of accounts/free plans/current prices. No provider-specific mandatory dependency.

## 5. CID-03 — Organization-managed providers and readiness

OAC → Administration → Communication and Delivery → Providers shows channel, provider/account, sender, config revision, readiness, last verified time, supported features, health, usage and callback capability without plaintext secrets. An authorized tenant administrator may add named profile(s), choose channel/adapter, configure approved endpoints, verify sender identity, supply write-only credentials, authorize OAuth, test and publish an immutable revision. Choose primary/default profile per purpose and channel within tenant policy; optional alternative profile not auto-selected unless reviewed route plan. Support disabled/draft/verified/active/draining/retired state with distinct credential and health dimensions. “Verified” is a test of a specific revision/account/sender/capability, not promise of eventual delivery. Changed host, sender, account, key or permissions invalidates applicable test. Test sends limited to separately verified operator-controlled destinations, synthetic text and non-live links, no real guest audience. Credential validation can be read-only where provider supports, then actual test send when enabling. No save/preview/test triggers live invitation.

Store settings non-secret allowlisted projection; never return password, API token, refresh token, webhook secret, encrypted blob or unmasked recipient in ordinary list. Credential revision/secret version and access controls separate; rotate/revoke without altering historic provider reference. If a pinned job uses a disabled/revoked credential or provider, block before submission; replan or reapprove route, never silently use unrelated sender. In-flight submissions may already have escaped; mark honest uncertain/accepted and reconcile. Provider removal/drain reviews queued jobs, scheduled campaigns, pending callbacks, suppression and active leases; do not delete evidence. Expired OAuth access or temporary rate-limit is not same as revoked provider access. Readiness includes domain verification, provider account status, credentials, permission scope, test, reachable endpoints, callback state and appropriate data processing review. Missing callback may allow accepted-only send if policy explicitly permits; never invent delivered. Health checks do not send unsolicited messages.

## 6. CID-04 — Platform-managed delivery services

PAC may optionally offer a shared provider service (for example operator-paid SMTP or email API) to explicitly assigned organizations. Organization-owned profiles remain independent. Platform service = service identity, allowed purpose/channel, provider and sender policy, revision, secret scope, quotas, cost/usage allocation, support/recovery policy. Tenant assignment = explicit active service grant with own routing profile, tenant-specific verified sender/domain or disclosed shared sender, allowed purpose/limits, start/end, billing/usage attribution, audit. Separate activation and assignment from tenant choosing to use it. No implicit entitlement via admin flag, hosting plan or OAC login. OAC sees own assignment/readiness/remaining quota only, not global credential/account ID that can route another tenant or other tenants' volume/content/destinations.

Prefer provider subaccounts/scoped keys and tenant-specific callback routing whenever supported; if shared account cannot reliably isolate callbacks, senders, suppressions, recipient evidence and quotas, do not support that deployment mode. Shared account provider event IDs may collide across provider accounts; use service/account namespace and exact correlation; ambiguous mapping quarantined with no disclosure. PAC can rotate/disable service or change limits with impact preview; active tenant grants revocation prevents new sends and discloses in-flight uncertainty. Only narrow broker/worker can retrieve permitted secret after validating tenant assignment, purpose, sender, destination policy and live lease. No cross-tenant secret reads from staff or general runtime. Platform operator's support diagnostics are aggregate/redacted, and sensitive tenant inspection requires explicit approved support under PAC policy and actual actor, expiry, audit; never silent impersonation. Cross-tenant abuse/bounce complaints investigated within authorized bounds. Shared suppression constraints handled without revealing other tenants' contacts (deny generic reason; service scope not public directory). Tenant cannot override provider/global abuse stop. Platform quotas not customer invoices; optional settlement separately designed, no charge execution.

## 7. CID-05 — Email SMTP and modern HTTP API

Preserve compatible SMTP with explicit TLS mode and server certificate verification; prefer authenticated submission or approved local relay. Password/app credential supported only when provider policy permits; no promise that consumer Gmail or Microsoft 365 password auth works. OAuth may authenticate SMTP (SASL XOAUTH2 where supported) or HTTP API; authentication and transport are separate settings. Add TDS text + sanitized HTML multipart, optional approved private generated PDF/ICS/inline approved asset where provider supports; always useful plaintext alternative. Do not send bearer credentials in headers, subject, attachment names or tracking data. One intended recipient per personalization, no bulk CC leaking guests; BCC operational generic notices only after reviewed policy, default one recipient. Avoid list headers disclosing recipient selection. Reply-to approved per organization/purpose; non-spoofable verified sender; domain SPF/DKIM/DMARC readiness documented and checked as applicable without exposing DNS changes as automatically made. Include provider-supported unsubscribe options for relevant consent-bound content, separate purpose policy and mandatory transactional notices. No tracking pixel default; open rates not read evidence. Bounce/complaint suppression scoped and audited.

HTTP adapters send a deterministic immutable request or approved provider template reference, validated envelope, recipient and content hashes, stable attempt/request reference; if provider supports idempotency, persist key before call and use only within documented validity and exact payload. Never retry an ambiguous acceptance after idempotency expiry on assumption safe. Provider receipt ID not global; accepted does not mean delivered. Retry only on definite rejection before acceptance, with bounded policies. HTTP timeouts, network reset after write, unknown response, partial batch outcome and worker crash after transmission are uncertain unless proven otherwise. Disable automatic POST retry/redirects by SDK unless reviewed. HTTP API key scope least privilege, webhook reconciliation per adapter. First reference HTTP adapter proposed Resend due to existing correlation knowledge; actual choice at M0, no mandate to use Resend commercially. Provider-specific callbacks reviewed rather than applying SMTP Resend webhook semantics globally.

## 8. CID-06 — OAuth account linking and secret lifecycle

Provide approved named OAuth integrations (not arbitrary token endpoint input). Use confidential server-side client, Authorization Code + PKCE where provider supports, exact redirect origins, cryptographically random single-use state bound to tenant, actor/session, provider, requested scope and expiry; validate issuer/tenant/account, redirect and token claims as applicable. OAuth callback must not switch Eventor workspace or expose another organization's account. Store refresh/access token only in authenticated encrypted secret boundary; short-lived access token in memory when possible; never browser storage, URLs/logs or editable settings. Scope send-only where available, no mailbox read or broad directory unless specifically approved and necessary. Service/client-credentials flow only where supported with explicit provider admin consent and allowed mailbox/resource grant; avoid blanket delegated access. Disconnect/revoke locally immediately; best-effort provider-side revocation separately reported. No OAuth as substitute for Eventor user session, no password reset or global membership created.

Serialize refresh per provider credential version with lease/CAS to avoid losing rotating refresh token; on invalid_grant or expired consent stop sends, show reauthorization required. Retry on definite pre-send auth expiry only after renewed same-account credentials and current permissions; never switch account or payload automatically. Token encryption key separation/rotation under existing key policy, no secret access via backup report. Restore must not resurrect revoked provider or old consent; reconcile credential epoch/revocations with operator before opening queues. Provider admin approval/account ownership changes require re-review. Audit link/refresh outcome/revoke with safe actor/account ref/scope and no secret. OAuth discovery endpoints fixed/allowlisted TLS, no arbitrary IdP URL. Test CSRF, stale state, account mix-up, replay, concurrent refresh, wrong tenant callback and revoked consent.

## 9. CID-07 — SMS delivery

Use official gateway adapters supporting target countries and sender registration. Normalize phone numbers with country context, preserve original encrypted value and explicit verification; do not guess country when ambiguous, do not treat phone identity as proven. Prefer per-recipient single message intent, provider segment calculation/limits and cost preview. GSM-7 extension table and Unicode/UCS-2 behavior must be tested against provider algorithm; Bosnian diacritics, long links, emoji and multi-segment messages may affect cost. Never silently transliterate a guest's name or split a secure URL. No sensitive details in SMS/lock screen; short approved personal link and minimal context. First-party token URL may be compact using opaque capability; never third-party public shortener with tracking or enumerable reference. Sender ID/short code/number and reply support country/provider specific; only approved registered sender. STOP/opt-out or provider suppression normalized and applied to correct contact/purpose scope, authenticated inbound callback; no free-form reply as RSVP unless separate reviewed verification/domain command. Receipts may be delayed, unavailable or partial; no read status. Cost cap by actual estimated segments and country; policy blocks uncertain/unpriced paid route unless bounded approved quote. No rerouting around carrier blocks or suppression.

## 10. CID-08 — WhatsApp official business messaging

Use official Business Platform or approved provider service only. Tenant-owned business identity/number and explicit verified sender assignment, account readiness, region/recipient eligibility, opt-in proof and approved template/category/language mapping where required. Provider rules and any conversation/time-window eligibility validated at review and send, not hardcoded timeless assumptions. Approved provider template version must match TDS mapped fields/locale and allowed purpose; template rejection/paused status stops send, not auto-switch to arbitrary text. Map text/media/button/URL parameters from typed purpose projection; approved attachment via private bounded provider upload or dedicated revocable short-lived link as supported. No shared public guest file URL. Do not transmit private floor plans, internal notes or unrelated contacts. No unofficial WhatsApp Web scraping, consumer personal session/token import, bulk phone probing or bypass of provider policy.

Inbound event scope: verified status events, opt-out, structured replies routed to minimal staff follow-up or approved domain verification. A WhatsApp number/chat receipt alone is not RSVP/booking acceptance; explicit verified Eventor capability and separate authoritative action required. Read receipt only if verifiable and allowed, never as legal acceptance. Provider fees/quality/quotas/template categories may change; readiness tracks current policy/estimate. Manual sharing via user-selected app remains separate from official business API send, no fabricated delivery tracking.

## 11. CID-09 — Viber and optional Telegram

Viber has distinct official Business Messages (where approved service can address eligible phone contacts) and bot interfaces (subscriber identifier and prior subscription). Maintain explicit adapter variants and destination kinds. Do not assume a phone number identifies a bot subscriber or that a business sender works in all countries. Register sender, verify service/contract requirements, capture opt-in, map approved message/template formats and callback semantics; normalize delivery/read only if supported. Validate rich media/URL constraints, country pricing and provider status before activation. No personal account automation or unofficial Viber desktop integration. Staff may record manual handoff without claiming Viber delivered/read.

Telegram optional Bot API: tenant-managed or explicitly granted bot identity, guest initiates conversation and binds verified chat via a one-time, purpose-limited enrollment challenge from authenticated portal/staff-assisted flow. Do not include live RSVP/admission token in bot enrollment URL; separate nonce bound to tenant/event/intended contact and expiry, explicit confirmation prevents wrong-chat linking. Store encrypted chat identifier scoped to bot/tenant/contact, user can disconnect, respect blocked/deleted chat. Never scan usernames, phone numbers or group members; private chat for personal content, group broadcast only approved public projection. Verify webhooks via supported secret mechanism or authenticated polling with durable update cursor; no arbitrary command execution. Bot send acceptance not proof of read. Optional extension can remain disabled with no effect on core workflow.

## 12. CID-10 — Web push and in-app delivery

Browser/PWA subscription only after contextual explanation and user permission; no coercive prompt on login, no requirement to accept for RSVP/booking. Bind subscription to authorized account or event-scoped verified guest context, browser/service worker/origin and purpose; explicitly confirm selection and support revoke/unsubscribe, expiry and rotation. Encrypt endpoint and key material, validate endpoint through approved push-service/egress policy, prevent SSRF from attacker-controlled subscriptions. VAPID key per deployment or isolated scope as reviewed; use standard payload encryption, key rotation and bounded TTL. Push message default generic (“Event information updated”), no name, private seat, credential, price or restricted details on lock screen. Clicking opens approved origin and revalidates current authority; not a raw token in notification body. Push provider acceptance, service-worker receipt (if specifically implemented) and human action are distinct; no read claim. Multiple device subscriptions and shared device removal; clear sensitive state on workspace switch, logout, guest expiry or kiosk reset. No guest push subscription on shared Info Pult by default.

In-app notification is an authorized projection of event/task/booking/communication state and separate personal read/ack marker. Use existing outbox/realtime cursor rules, no global data broadcast, revalidate on reconnect and revoke. A read marker is not approval or acceptance. Public programme signage is publication through AGIS/TDS, not a push subscription or personal delivery. Offline local operation may show current reachable-server notices even if external push fails; no dependency on browser vendor service for core.

## 13. CID-11 — Channel-independent invitation and capability distribution

Refactor only the owning invitation command boundary when scheduled. Separate issuance of one invitation revision and one appropriate purpose-bound access capability from preparation of zero or more distribution jobs/tasks. Preserve current explicit issue-and-email command/API behavior through an adapter; add explicit issue-without-email and reviewed distribution choices. A person with no electronic contact can receive a printed invitation or a staff-recorded invitation and answer by phone or in person. ## 18. CID-16 — Routing, fallback, retry policy and failure semantics

Define channel choice algorithm as auditable deterministic plan, not provider SDK magic. Route is a specific channel + verified destination version + provider/account/config/sender + content revision + policy. Alternative provider for same channel is still a new route requiring explicit approval in plan; cannot circumvent suppression/region/template requirement. Do not silently switch sender/account or convert template. If no eligible route, create authorized follow-up obligation (not fake send). Manual or print/phone may be primary route, not just fallback.

| Observed condition | Automatic behavior (if reviewed policy allows) |
|---|---|
| Before submission, definite transient connection failure | Retry same immutable route with bounded backoff; only if evidence proves no external side effect. |
| Definite provider rejection without acceptance, retryable | Honor Retry-After/rate policy and expiry; retry same route or permitted alternative per reviewed plan. |
| Confirmed terminal non-delivery (e.g. verified bounce) | Suppress invalid contact as appropriate; consider preapproved alternate contact/channel only after rechecking policy/consent, cost and purpose. |
| Provider accepted, delivery unknown or read unknown | No automatic fallback merely due to absence of receipt/read. Reconcile or await bounded evidence; separately approved escalation can deliberately risk another communication and must disclose. |
| Timeout after submit, crash, partial response, unknown external effect | Mark uncertain; stable correlation, callback/poll/manual review. Do not blind retry or fallback. | Opt-out/suppression, revoked actor/credential/tenant grant | Block protected send; no alternate route to circumvent restriction. Another independently permissible contact route may be selected only under reviewed lawful purpose, not to evade channel/global opt-out. |
| Expired/stale domain revision, link revoked, provider template rejected | Block; require domain correction/new review, never mutate invitation via adapter. |
| Quota/budget exhausted or price changed | Stop new claims/reservations; review cost/replan. No unapproved charge. |
| Operator cancels before handoff | Record cancelled/suppressed remaining; release unused reservation; in-flight may still deliver. |

Retries: bounded attempt count (existing queues max five preserved until reviewed successor), exponential backoff + jitter with provider-specific retry-after and global/tenant limiter, not fixed aggressive polling. Dead-letter/manual review after expiry or exhaustion, do not infinite requeue. Classify permanent/transient/uncertain with adapter evidence and safe code. Store prior attempts immutable; a retry is new attempt of same approved intent, stable logical intent and provider idempotency scope as appropriate. A new manual resend requires reviewed reason, current authority, fresh cost reservation and separate logical intent to avoid confusion; may reuse valid domain capability, never revoke/replace implicitly. Fallback decision itself durable, idempotent (one active next route per intent), audited, parent attempt/evidence and rule revision, with compare-and-swap against late callback and cancellation. If verified late success appears after alternate route issued, preserve both facts, stop remaining chain, notify operator of potential duplicate; do not erase history. For multi-channel intentional delivery, counts reflect subjects vs sends and cost; require explicit approval. Keep operational exception queue scoped and redacted. Never use vendor fallback to bypass provider rate/abuse enforcement.

## 21. CID-19 — Provider adapter and shared technical architecture

API-first modular monolith. Vue feature modules `features/communications` and existing domain views use shared components/composables per ADR 0008; Fastify transport-neutral orchestration and domain-specific command services; PostgreSQL forced RLS and narrow security-definer functions, restricted credential store, transactional outbox and tenant queue discovery; existing bounded worker processes plus transport adapters; isolated TDS renderer receives immutable sanitized inputs and no provider credentials or arbitrary network; callback ingestion verifies then normalizes under restricted worker identity. No new mandatory message broker, Redis, microservice, commercial control plane or background browser automation. Can scale workers independently using DB-backed lease and fair tenant claim policy. Provider integrations are reviewed installed code with versioned manifests, not user-uploadable plugin scripts. API and worker roles never have owner/superuser or cross-tenant raw credential table access. Host bootstrap/recovery protected and deployment-neutral.

Illustrative TypeScript contract (not code to merge):

```ts
interface DeliveryAdapter {
  readonly adapterId: string;
  readonly contractVersion: number;
  capabilities(profile: SafeProfile): Promise<CapabilityReport>;
  validateConfiguration(profile: SafeProfile, secret: ScopedSecretHandle): Promise<ValidationResult>;
  estimate(request: ImmutableDeliveryEnvelope): Promise<BoundedEstimate>;
  submit(request: ImmutableDeliveryEnvelope, grant: ShortLivedSubmissionGrant): Promise<SubmissionEvidence>;
  reconcile?(reference: ScopedProviderReference, grant: ShortLivedReadGrant): Promise<VerifiedEvidence[]>;
  verifyCallback?(raw: BoundedRawRequest, context: ScopedCallbackContext): Promise<VerifiedEvidence[]>;
}
```

Adapter cannot select another recipient, query Registry, issue invitation/token, change event/booking/task or call arbitrary API. Envelope includes opaque tenant/intent/attempt IDs, exact provider/account/sender/config revision, purpose, approved destination ciphertext handle resolved only at last boundary, subject/body/typed template or private artifact handle, locale, content digest, stable idempotency ref, expiry, route/cost authorization and minimal correlation. Grant binds worker lease/tenant/account/purpose/attempt/deadline; narrow secret service releases credentials only for exact approved profile and revalidated authority. Adapter return `definiteAccepted`, `definiteRejected` with retryability, `uncertain`, `preSubmissionBlocked` and normalized provider reference/evidence, not generic exception equals retry. Capability/test operation never issues real domain message; test is separately authorized synthetic intent. For providers lacking idempotency guarantee, application still deduplicates local intents and uses uncertain behavior; exactly-once external delivery is not promised. Batch submit only if per-recipient correlation, idempotency, partial outcomes and no recipient leakage are proven; otherwise one logical submission per recipient. Provider SDK retry defaults disabled/reviewed, pinned versions, sandbox no real secrets in CI. Any new adapter requires capability/limitation docs, threat model, tests, rate/cost policy, account setup/revocation, callback/polling evidence and operator recovery guide before exposure in UI.

Transactional path: owning command validates domain actor/role, selects revision and creates intent/outbox in same transaction (or records obligation for reviewed later communication). Scheduler computes due items under approved rule; worker discovers tenant IDs only through narrow queue, sets tenant context, claims ready attempt with lease/fence and reserves quota/cost atomically using consistent reviewed lock order (domain/permission/policy → campaign/intent → budget → lease as owning invariants require; audit actual lock ordering in M0). Decrypt minimal content and validate current authority, capability, suppression, expiry and current approved artifact; prepare stable external correlation and mark durable pre-handoff/submitting boundary before network. Recheck cancellation/revocation under existing send-authorization locks/fencing; don't hold broad DB transaction across unbounded network, review exact linearization point and bounded handoff; revocation before authorized handoff blocks, already-started external submission cannot be recalled. Completion validates lease fence and appends evidence/settles reservation, stale worker cannot overwrite newer outcome. Crash after boundary uncertain. ## 24. CID-22 — Security, privacy, abuse prevention and governance

Threat model includes tenant leakage, malicious sender or operator, compromised provider account, forged callbacks, arbitrary destination/SSRF, secret exposure, token forwarding, phishing content, duplicate submissions, consent abuse, runaway charges, cross-tenant service quota starvation, queue poisoning, stale scheduled authority and restore resurrection. Apply existing project security architecture and accepted ADRs; no blanket weakening for easier onboarding.

- Transport: TLS with hostname/cert validation, no disable verification or plaintext credential downgrade. Do not allow arbitrary SMTP envelope, header injection, executable templates or user-selected unrestricted API target.
- Credentials: separate tenant/provider/service secret boundaries; encrypted authenticated context binding, per-kind key policy and rotation; no secret visible to ordinary API, tenant admin after saving, logs, metrics, SQL debug, CI, browser cache, backup manifest or support export. Limit decrypt to actual claim/account, no global secrets table access. Scoped OAuth, short-lived grants, revoke/rotate with verified sender account, new revision readiness and safe drain. Avoid sending secrets by email or test notification. Secret leak incident stops affected profile, rotates key, reconciles outstanding jobs and records actual operator; already escaped messages can't be recalled.
- Personal data: purpose-minimize contact fields, content and callback storage; keep live capability secret only narrow encrypted delivery/render boundary; subject/headers must not contain access secret, sensitive reason, medical/restricted info. Redact structured logs and safe failure taxonomy, no raw provider payload default. Staff may reveal masked recipient only exact purpose/authority and audit. Avoid sending internal restricted map/Registry/contract data to public channel. - Consent and anti-abuse: tenant purpose-specific consent/suppression and opt-out, approved sender, per-purpose audience caps, verified provider account and recipient eligibility, no arbitrary contact harvest, no credential forwarding links to unrelated domains, no generic external mail relay. Append-only approval, attempt and evidence; corrections append. No fake provider status or human impersonation. Provider IDs namespace, replay and out-of-order reconciliation, conflicting payload quarantine. Incident drill verifies credential and contact isolation, no token in logs or artifacts not authorized. Operational metrics contain no guest name, phone, message content or live secret. Exports require current authorization and retention/expiry.

Retention decision must specify per-purpose content, address/recipient snapshot, provider metadata, rendered artifact, callback quarantine, consent proof, financial usage and audit windows and lawful hold, deletion actor and backup expiry; actual legal retention rules not inferred. Offboarding disables new sends/grants/subscriptions, reviews outstanding tasks/leases/unknown outcomes and authorizes portability within scope; secret revoke/erase according to policy, provider-side deletion where supported only reported when evidenced. No automatic business cancellation/refund or destruction of audit on offboarding.
